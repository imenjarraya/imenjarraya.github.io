Teaching statement

From principles to working systems

Approximately eight years of university teaching, laboratory instruction, student mentoring, and graduation-project supervision. My teaching connects rigorous theoretical foundations with practical engineering implementation — guiding students from understanding to designing, testing, validating, documenting, and communicating complete technical solutions.

My teaching practice is grounded in my dual identity as an electrical engineer and an active researcher in embedded intelligent systems, robotics, IoT, energy management, and autonomous systems. My academic teaching has covered real-time systems, embedded systems programming, IoT, industrial computing, electronics, electrotechnics, automation, electrical engineering workshops, and biomedical measurement — across the Higher Institute of Technological Studies of Sidi Bouzid, the Higher Institute of Industrial Management of Sfax, and the Higher Institute of Biotechnology of Sfax. These appointments allowed me to teach students with diverse levels of preparation and to adapt my pedagogy to both classroom and laboratory environments.

Teaching philosophy

My main objective is to prepare students to become independent, rigorous, creative, and ethically responsible engineers. I do not view teaching as the simple transfer of information. It is a structured learning process through which students learn how to reason, model, implement, debug, evaluate, and improve engineering systems. For this reason I combine lectures, tutorials, laboratory work, simulations, mini-projects, and final-year projects, and I encourage students to ask questions, test assumptions, analyze errors, and justify their technical choices using scientific reasoning.

In technical courses I begin with fundamental principles and progressively connect them to real applications. In embedded systems and IoT, students first study microcontroller architecture, sensors, actuators, communication protocols, and data acquisition, then implement prototypes on Arduino, Raspberry Pi, STM32, and ESP32. In automation and electrotechnics, I connect theoretical models to laboratory measurements, wiring practice, control logic, safety, and troubleshooting. Engineering knowledge becomes meaningful when it is verified through implementation and experimental validation.

I emphasize learning by doing: students make technical decisions, compare implementation choices, evaluate limitations, and interpret unexpected results. This builds confidence and transforms practical sessions from procedural exercises into opportunities for engineering reasoning.

My laboratory teaching is based on progressive autonomy. At the start of a session I provide the scientific background, objectives, safety instructions, expected outcomes, and evaluation criteria. During the session students are guided to connect components, configure software tools, acquire measurements, interpret results, and identify possible sources of error. By the end they present their work in a technical report covering objective, methodology, experimental setup, results, discussion, and conclusion.

Project-based learning and supervision

Project-based learning is central to my practice. I have supervised and co-supervised graduation projects in IoT, AI, robotics, photovoltaic systems, electronics, automation, and embedded systems — including intelligent airport intrusion monitoring (IoT + AI), connected mailbox design, surveillance and security drones, Arduino-based PV acquisition systems, electronic cards for monitoring physical quantities in PV installations, and charge regulators for autonomous PV systems.

During supervision I guide students through the full engineering development cycle: defining the problem, identifying system requirements, reviewing the state of the art, selecting components, designing hardware or software architectures, implementing prototypes, testing performance, interpreting results, and preparing written and oral presentations. I place strong emphasis on clear methodology, reproducibility, technical documentation, and professional communication. My role is to help students become autonomous while ensuring their work remains scientifically coherent and technically feasible.

  • I help students transform broad project ideas into precise engineering objectives, functional specifications, and measurable deliverables.
  • I encourage the use of block diagrams, flowcharts, experimental protocols, validation tables, and clear result interpretation.
  • I support students in writing structured reports and preparing professional presentations for academic juries.
  • I promote teamwork, responsibility, ethical engineering practice, and respect for safety procedures in laboratory and project environments.

Assessment and feedback

My assessment combines continuous evaluation, laboratory performance, technical reporting, oral communication, and project outcomes. In practical courses I evaluate not only the final result but the student’s process: preparation, problem understanding, instrument use, implementation quality, interpretation of measurements, debugging capacity, and clarity of the report. Engineering quality depends on both the final prototype and the reasoning behind its design.

Feedback is specific, constructive, and focused on improvement. When students make mistakes I use them as learning opportunities — asking them to identify the cause, propose corrections, and compare expected and observed results. In laboratory and project modules I value progress as much as outcomes, encouraging students to document intermediate results, record failed attempts, justify design changes, and explain the limitations of their work.

Integration of research into teaching

My teaching is enriched by my research in UAV navigation, robotics, embedded AI, energy management, battery health monitoring, and IoT-based intelligent systems. Sensor fusion concepts can be explained through UAV localization in GNSS-denied environments; embedded programming connects to real-time robotics; energy conversion concepts link to hybrid electric vehicles, batteries, supercapacitors, and renewable energy. This research-informed teaching helps students see the connection between academic courses and real technological challenges.

Mentoring, inclusion, and learning environment

I aim to create a respectful, motivating, and inclusive classroom environment. Because students do not all learn in the same way, I use multiple forms of explanation — mathematical derivations, block diagrams, simulations, hardware demonstrations, code examples, practical case studies — and I encourage peer learning, discussion, and collaborative problem solving. Mentoring is one of the most meaningful aspects of my role: I support students not only in technical development but in building confidence, discipline, scientific curiosity, and professional behavior.

Future teaching goals

I aim to develop modern, research-oriented teaching modules in embedded systems, robotics, IoT, AI, renewable energy, and autonomous systems. I am particularly interested in creating laboratory activities that integrate ROS 2, UAV simulation, embedded AI, sensor fusion, smart monitoring, and energy management — supported by clear diagrams, structured laboratory guides, real datasets, and open-source tools that allow students to reproduce and extend their experiments.

My goal is to prepare students who can combine theory, practice, creativity, and ethical responsibility — engineers who can analyze complex systems, implement reliable solutions, communicate results clearly, and continue learning independently.
