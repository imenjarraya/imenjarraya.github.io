Research statement

Reliable, deployable intelligence for autonomous systems

My research is positioned at the intersection of electrical engineering, embedded computer science, robotics, artificial intelligence, and intelligent energy systems. I build methods that are mathematically grounded, computationally efficient, and suitable for implementation on embedded or robotic platforms.

🔬 View Research PDF See publications

A defining characteristic of my research profile is the integration of theoretical modeling, data-driven learning, sensor fusion, simulation, and experimental validation. My work therefore combines algorithm design with practical engineering constraints — sensor uncertainty, computation time, hardware limitations, environmental variability, and real-time operation. My trajectory has evolved from hybrid electric vehicle energy management and battery modeling toward autonomous UAV navigation, GNSS-denied localization, embedded AI, robotics, and IoT-based sensing systems.

Research vision

My long-term research vision is to contribute to autonomous and intelligent systems that can operate safely and accurately in uncertain, constrained, and infrastructure-limited environments — UAVs, mobile robots, embedded AI systems, and energy-aware intelligent platforms. These systems require robust perception, localization, prediction, control, and decision-making methods that remain reliable when conventional assumptions fail: when GNSS is degraded or unavailable, when sensor measurements are noisy, when environmental maps are incomplete.

To address these challenges I pursue a research strategy that combines physics-based modeling, machine learning, deep learning, and sensor fusion. Purely model-based methods may struggle in complex nonlinear environments; purely data-driven models may lack interpretability, robustness, or generalization. The goal is hybrid methods that draw on both: the structure and reliability of engineering models, and the adaptability of modern AI.

UAV localization and navigation in GNSS-denied environments

My recent research has focused strongly on autonomous UAV localization and navigation under degraded or unavailable GNSS conditions — a problem critical to real-world UAV operation in urban, mountainous, indoor, contested, or infrastructure-limited environments. In these scenarios, conventional satellite navigation may be unreliable, and the UAV must estimate its position using onboard sensors, environmental maps, and intelligent fusion algorithms.

I have contributed to UAV localization frameworks that integrate inertial measurements, LiDAR or altitude-related information, barometric data, visual or non-visual sensing, and georeferenced terrain information such as DEM, DSM, and GeoTIFF maps. The methods include terrain-aided navigation, TERCOM-inspired matching, EKF-based fusion, residual learning, and AI-enhanced localization models — all aimed at reducing localization drift and improving position recovery during GNSS-denied intervals.

A major direction is the design of hybrid learning and filtering architectures: CNN-LSTM-Attention models, Kolmogorov-Arnold Network-based components, GRU predictors, XGBoost residual learning, and EKF-based correction mechanisms. These exploit temporal sensor patterns, elevation information, motion dynamics, and terrain constraints, providing localization that is accurate, robust, and suitable for autonomous navigation in realistic outdoor environments.

Open-source dataset generation and simulation-based validation

A second major direction is reproducible research infrastructure for UAV localization and tracking. I have contributed to open-source frameworks that use ROS 2, PX4, Gazebo, QGIS, Blender, and georeferenced terrain data to generate UAV datasets and validate autonomous navigation algorithms. Progress in GNSS-denied navigation depends not only on algorithm design but on high-quality datasets, realistic simulation, and transparent evaluation.

My work in this direction includes UAV mission simulation, georeferenced data collection, synchronized sensor recording, trajectory generation, DEM/DSM-based terrain preprocessing, and experimental validation workflows. By combining simulation and real-world-inspired sensing modalities, I seek to narrow the gap between algorithmic development and practical deployment — especially through benchmark environments that allow researchers to compare methods under controlled GNSS-denial scenarios, different terrain profiles, sensor noise levels, and trajectories.

Robotics, tracking, and embedded artificial intelligence

Beyond UAV localization, I work on intelligent robotic systems that combine sensing, prediction, and decision-making — aerial target tracking, drone-to-drone tracking, trajectory prediction, and the integration of Kalman filtering with neural sequence models. These systems require fast inference, temporal reasoning, and robustness to motion uncertainty, occlusion, and dynamic environmental conditions.

Within RIOTU I also participate in research on quadruped robotics, embedded AI, and autonomous decision-making. The broader objective is robotic systems that can perceive their environment, estimate their state, predict future motion, and act reliably under complex conditions.

Embedded systems, wireless communication, and industrial AI

Alongside academic research, I have developed strong practical expertise in the design and programming of embedded systems for robotics, IoT, and AI-based industrial solutions. At Novel-Ti I worked on AI-enabled applications including facial recognition, motion detection for children’s interactive games, and computer-vision-based ball detection for VAR-style applications — combining embedded programming, image processing, real-time constraints, and applied AI in product-oriented contexts.

I have also contributed to wireless-communication projects (Zigbee, LoRa, ESP32, SIM8008), PCB design with Altium Designer, temperature and humidity monitoring in beehive environments, and a thermistor-based food monitoring system for restaurant kitchens. This gives me a practical grasp of the trade-offs between short-range, long-range, and cellular communication modules — directly applicable to smart monitoring and cloud-connected data acquisition across agricultural and food-production chains, including olive-oil quality monitoring.

Energy management, battery modeling, and intelligent power systems

My doctoral work established my foundation in hybrid electric vehicle energy management, focused on systems combining Li-ion batteries and supercapacitors. It involved battery and supercapacitor modeling, SOC estimation, recursive least squares identification, optimization, power-management strategies, and hardware-in-the-loop validation using MATLAB, Simulink, dSPACE, and embedded test benches.

The line continues through battery state estimation and health monitoring: AI-enhanced battery modeling, SOC and SOH prediction, uncertainty-aware estimation, and hybrid neural architectures such as KAN-LSTM models. These methods matter for electric vehicles, renewable energy storage, smart grids, and energy-aware robotic systems. The long-term objective is reliable and interpretable models that support safer, more efficient energy management.

Methodological approach

My methodology follows a complete engineering cycle: define the problem and identify the physical constraints, sensor limitations, and system requirements; develop mathematical models; design algorithms; implement them in software; and validate through simulation or experimental datasets. I emphasize quantitative evaluation — RMSE, MAE, R², inference time, robustness under noise, trajectory deviation, computational complexity.

I also value reproducibility and transparency: open-source software, documented pipelines, benchmark datasets, clear ablation studies, and well-defined evaluation protocols. Strong research must provide not only good results but a clear explanation of why a method works, under which conditions it performs well, and where its limitations remain.

Future agenda

1 · Robust autonomous localization. Hybrid frameworks for UAVs and mobile robots that combine terrain-aided navigation, sensor fusion, physics-guided learning, uncertainty estimation, and real-time embedded deployment — moving from offline evaluation toward real-time validation on robotic platforms.

2 · Embedded AI for autonomous decision-making. Lightweight, interpretable learning architectures for robotics: KAN-based models, attention mechanisms, reinforcement learning, and agentic decision-making frameworks. Beyond accuracy, the focus is on reliability, explainability, and deployment feasibility on Jetson platforms, microcontrollers, and edge devices.

3 · Intelligent energy-aware autonomy. Connecting electric-vehicle energy management and battery health monitoring with robotics and UAVs: battery-aware mission planning, energy-constrained navigation, health-aware power management, and intelligent monitoring for mobile robots and UAVs.

Collaboration, mentoring, scientific impact

My research is strongly connected to collaboration, mentoring, and dissemination. The challenges addressed in autonomous systems, robotics, IoT, and energy management require interdisciplinary work, and I have contributed to projects, journal publications, conference papers, open-source software, student mentoring, and scientific activities at RIOTU. As a researcher and university teacher, I consider mentoring an essential part of my mission — guiding students and young researchers in defining research questions, building experimental pipelines, analyzing results, writing scientific papers, and developing professional research habits.
